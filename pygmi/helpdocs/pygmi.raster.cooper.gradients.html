Gradient Calculation

Options

    The gradient calculation calculates different derivatives of the data. It allows for the calculation of a conventional horizontal derivative, a derivative ratio (ratio of x and y derivatives), a vertical derivative and a total horizontal gradient. The algorithms are based on Cooper and Cowan (2006, 2007). The input dataset must be in a projection, not in geographic/geodetic coordinates.
  • Radiobutton choice of Directional Derivative, Derivative Ratio, Vertical Derivative or Total Horizontal Gradient.
  • Azimuth - Available for directional derivative and derivative ratio. This is the direction of the derivative, in the plane of the data. Features perpendicular to the azimuth are highlighted, where 0 degrees is E-W, 45 degrees is NE-SW, -45 degrees is NW-SE and 90 degrees is N-S.
  • Strength Factor - This is the strength of the derivative ratio. A value of 1 is recommended as a starting point.

References

Cooper,G.R.J., Cowan, D.R. 2007. Enhancing linear features in image data using horizontal orthogonal gradient ratios. Computers and Geosciences, 33, p.981-984

Cooper,G.R.J., Cowan, D.R. 2006. Enhancing potential field data using filters based on the local phase. Computers and Geosciences, 32(10), 1585–1591.
